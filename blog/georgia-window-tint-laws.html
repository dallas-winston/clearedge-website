Georgia Window Tint Laws: What's Legal in 2026

By Dallas Eberle · May 3, 2026

Georgia requires at least 32% visible light transmission (VLT) on front side windows for passenger vehicles. Rear side windows and the rear windshield also require 32% VLT. The windshield allows only a non-reflective tint strip on the top 6 inches. Here's the full breakdown.

Legal VLT Limits for Passenger Vehicles in Georgia

Window Minimum VLT Allowed Notes
Windshield Top 6 inches only Non-reflective tint strip permitted above AS-1 line
Front side windows 32% VLT Must allow at least 32% of light through
Rear side windows 32% VLT Same as front for sedans and coupes
Rear windshield 32% VLT Same limit applies

SUVs and vans have more flexibility on rear windows. Georgia allows any darkness on rear side windows and the rear windshield for multi-purpose vehicles (SUVs, vans, trucks with rear seating). The 32% VLT rule still applies to the front side windows.

What Does VLT Mean?

VLT stands for Visible Light Transmission, the percentage of visible light that passes through the tint film. A 32% VLT means 32% of light gets through and 68% is blocked. Lower VLT numbers mean darker tint.

For context: factory-tinted rear windows on most new cars are around 15–20% VLT. That factory tint on the rear is legal in Georgia, but applying aftermarket tint that dark on the front side windows is not.

What About Tint Reflectivity?

Georgia restricts reflective (mirror-like) tint. Front side windows cannot be more than 20% reflective. Rear windows follow the same 20% reflectivity limit. Highly mirrored tint films are not legal regardless of their VLT.

Medical Exemptions

Georgia does allow medical exemptions for darker tint. If you have a medical condition that requires reduced light exposure (lupus, photosensitivity, melanoma, etc.), you can apply for an exemption through the Georgia Department of Public Safety.

Requirements for a medical exemption:

  • A signed statement from a licensed physician
  • The statement must specify the medical condition and the VLT percentage needed
  • You must carry the exemption documentation in the vehicle at all times

What Are the Penalties for Illegal Tint in Georgia?

Driving with illegal tint in Georgia is a misdemeanor. Penalties include:

  • First offense: Fine up to $1,000 and/or up to 12 months in jail (though jail time is extremely rare for tint violations)
  • Fix-it ticket: Some officers issue a warning with a deadline to remove or replace the illegal tint
  • Vehicle inspection: Tint may be checked during state inspections in certain counties

In practice, most tint tickets result in a fine of $100–$200 plus the cost of removing and replacing the tint to legal specifications. It's cheaper to install legal tint from the start.

What Tint Levels Look Like in Practice

VLT percentages can be hard to visualize. Here's what common levels look like:

  • 50% VLT: Very light, barely noticeable from outside, slight glare reduction inside
  • 35% VLT: Medium, visible tint, good privacy, legal in most states but not Georgia front windows
  • 32% VLT: Georgia's legal limit for front windows, noticeable tint with decent privacy
  • 20% VLT: Dark, hard to see inside, common factory rear tint, illegal on front in Georgia
  • 5% VLT: "Limo tint," nearly opaque, illegal on all windows in Georgia except rear on SUVs/vans

Why Ceramic Tint Matters More Than Shade

Most people focus on how dark they can go. The smarter question is: what type of film are you using? Ceramic tint at 32% VLT (legal limit) outperforms cheap dyed tint at 5% VLT in the categories that actually matter:

  • Heat rejection: Ceramic tint rejects 40–60% of infrared heat. Dyed tint blocks almost none.
  • UV protection: Ceramic blocks 99% of UV rays. Dyed tint blocks significantly less.
  • Clarity: Ceramic maintains optical clarity with no haze or color shift. Dyed tint can look purple or hazy over time.
  • Longevity: Ceramic tint lasts 10+ years without fading. Dyed tint often degrades in 3–5 years.

In Georgia's summers, where interior temperatures can hit 150°F+ in a parked car, the heat rejection alone makes ceramic tint worth the upgrade. You stay legal and stay cooler.
